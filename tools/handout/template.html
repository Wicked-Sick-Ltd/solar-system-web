One thing to be clear about

This is astronomy, not astrology.

No horoscopes or natal charts - just observational data from NASA/JPL and the IAU, presented plainly and kept current.

Where the planets are - {{DATE_HUMAN}}

Eight worlds, model snapshot

Each dial looks down on the Solar System from above the Sun’s north pole. The dotted line is the planet’s own orbit drawn to its true shape; the small grey dot marks perihelion, its closest point to the Sun. Angles are heliocentric ecliptic longitude, measured anticlockwise from the March equinox direction (0°). Dials are not to a common scale - each orbit is drawn to fill its own dial. This sheet shows a two-body model for the date above, not a precision ephemeris - re-run tools/handout/generate.py at any time for a current one.

Then and now - 1991 reference vs catalogue snapshot

Moons then and now

When Deuteros shipped on the Amiga in 1991, the eight planets listed here had sixty known moons. Pluto and Charon are outside this eight-planet comparison. The snapshot counts below come from the selected catalogue; the 1991 column is the historical reference. A partial catalogue can omit known moons.

How this was counted

Snapshot figures are the selected catalogue’s own, read from the API at generation time. The 1991 column is the historical record as it stood at the end of that year. Two moons contribute to the difference despite older discovery dates: Jupiter’s Themisto was seen in 1975, lost, and not recovered until 2000; Uranus’s Perdita was imaged by Voyager 2 in 1986 but went unnoticed until 1999 and was not confirmed until 2003. Neither was a known moon in 1991.

Moon counts move. New outer satellites are announced regularly, so treat this page as a dated snapshot like the last one.
